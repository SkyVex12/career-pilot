# Standup, 2026-10-26: addressee, content and post-call analysis

The first real standup run through the app at a company workspace: session
`<session id>`, started 15:00:07 UTC, 38.6 min, Zoom, desktop app
0.1.2, against the Stage 0 GCP deployment (main at `f6e0aed`), OpenAI routes (gpt-6-luna for the
detector, fast pass and summary; gpt-6.1-sol for the deep pass), Deepgram Nova-3. Transcript
consent was on. This document still quotes no meeting text and names no teammate: findings are
paraphrased, and the repository is not the place for colleagues' words.

## Summary

The call was a standup in name only. For about 30 minutes a teammate presented a design and a
manager questioned them about it; the user spoke for under a minute of transcribed speech until
the status questions near the end. The app's two status answers (33:16 and 33:20) fired on the
right questions with pack-grounded content. Everything around them went wrong:

- **5 of the 7 questions the detector fired on were for the presenter, not the user.** The
  remote side is one "Them" channel, so a manager-presenter exchange looks like a one-on-one,
  and the user's name in the app ("[display name]") is not what teammates call them. When the
  manager did use the user's work name, the detector did not know it was the user, and one
  answer suggested "bringing questions to" the user as if they were someone else.
- **The Ask box was used for commands.** Three of four forced questions were typed instructions
  ("answer" twice, "answer every questions now"), each answered literally; the second "answer"
  came from the exact-repeat cache, a six-minute-old reply.
- **Standup content was grounded but hard to use:** prepared points were whole work-item
  summaries (200+ characters, some narrating "the user requested... Claude suppressed..."),
  "yesterday" was Thursday's log on a Monday (no Friday log), and one of the two logs in the
  last week never reached memory: its extraction failed when the OpenAI account ran out of
  credits on 2026-10-22 and the log has said "extracting" since.
- **The post-call report has no dates:** the request carries no session date, so both
  commitments have an empty due date, and their text ("the session date was not provided",
  a misheard report name) went straight into the next standup pack.

Latency was as before (fast first word at the client 1.7 s p50 / 2.1 s p95; the detector is the
floor). There were no errors after the first two minutes, no reconnects and one echo line. The
call cost about $0.41, **about $0.64 per live hour against the $0.84 estimate**, but the
detector ran about 930 times an hour and held fast passes about 400 times an hour, against the
250 and 20 the estimate assumes: cheap on gpt-6-luna, not on Haiku.

## 3. Standup checks

**Addressee.** Precision 2 of 7 on detected questions; recall 2 of 3 on questions to the user
(the third is the missed "remember" question). Three causes, all visible in code:

1. `DETECTOR_SYSTEM` has the right rule for group calls (only the user's name, "you" after the
   user spoke, or their turn), but the transcript labels every remote speaker "Them", and the
   detector is not told that a standup or meeting is a group call. A question from "Them"
   followed by an answer from "Them" reads as a one-on-one.
2. `{user_name}` is the profile's display name, "[display name]", which nobody in the call says. The
   user's work name appears in the call three times; once it was used to address the user.
3. Deepgram diarization is off, so the gateway cannot tell remote speakers apart either.

**Content.** The status answers came from the company's pack and cited real nodes; nothing was
invented. But:

- The pack was built in time (14:13 UTC, 47 min before; it is rebuilt on every memory change).
- "Yesterday" was 2026-10-22, a Thursday: there is no log for Friday 2026-10-23. Answers said
  "Yesterday:" anyway.
- The 2026-10-21 log never reached memory. `worklog.extract` failed five times on 2026-10-22
  with `AllRoutesFailedError` (OpenAI: no credits remaining). `extract_worklog` only marks the
  log `failed` on `ModelOutputError`; a provider error leaves it `extracting` forever, nothing
  retries it once the provider is back, and the Work screen polls every 3 s while any log is
  `extracting`.
- Prepared standup points are the nodes' `text`, the full extracted summary: 16 "yesterday"
  items, each 150 to 300 characters, some in the third person about "the user" and "Claude"
  (the logs are generated reports of the user's coding-assistant sessions, and extraction kept
  their narration). The 4-point prepared answer takes about 30 seconds to read.
- Q10 ("the plan this week") got yesterday/today/blockers: the `standup_status` shape has no
  case for a week question, and the pack's `recent` (rest of the week) was empty.

**Conversation memory.** The user said one substantive line (the plan for the week). Q11's fast
pass used it; Q11's deep pass repeated the pack's "today" instead. 16 summary calls ran (1.2k
in, 450 out on average, 3 to 8 s each, off the answer path); **5 of 16 ended at exactly 500
output tokens**, the cap, which on OpenAI includes reasoning tokens, so those summaries were
probably cut off at their end (the newest notes). Their text is gone, so this cannot be checked.

**Post-call report.** Ready 6 s after the end. Fair on substance (the user did not give a
"yesterday" and stated no blocker), but: `postcall_request` gets the user, mode, length and
company, **not the date**, so "today or tomorrow" became an empty due date and the model wrote
"the session date was not provided" into a commitment; a teammate's audio trouble was listed as
the user's blocker; and the next report's name was misheard (Deepgram's `keyterm` support exists
in `stt/deepgram.py` but no caller passes any terms, so the company's terms never reach STT).
Both commitments were stored as written and are now in the next standup pack's open
commitments.

## 5. Ranked issues

Ranked by effect on the user in a real standup.

### 1. Answers fire for teammates in group calls

- **Evidence:** 5 of 7 detected questions were the manager's questions to the presenter; the
  user's work name was used to address them and not recognized (section 3).
- **Root cause:** one "Them" label for every remote speaker; the detector is not told a standup
  or meeting is a group call; the user's name in prompts is the account's display name.
- **Update after review:** the session ran under the account's own profile by mistake; the
  meetings belong to the linked Career-OS user's profile, whose name is the one teammates say.
  So no separate "name at work": the prompts keep using the selected profile's name, now with
  its first name as the match. Two more needs came out of the review: remarks to the user that
  are not questions (the reminder at 35:23 was one) need a suggested reply too, and the user
  wants a button to ask for one.
- **Fix:** (a) in standup and meeting modes, a detector rule that "Them" is several people,
  something is for the user only by name (full or first), "you" right after the user spoke,
  or their turn, a question that follows someone else's presentation or answer is that
  person's, and a mention is not being addressed; (b) a detector type `respond` for remarks to
  the user that need a reply, with a short reply shape; (c) a Respond button next to Ask on
  the live panel (`question.force` kind `respond`); (d) later, Deepgram diarization on the
  "them" stream ("Them 1 / Them 2"; needs a price check and a protocol field; not in this
  branch).
- **Expected gain:** most of the 5 false fires gone (each is two model passes and a distracting
  overlay); the "remember?" turn gets a reply, by the detector or the button.
- **Risk:** more misses when someone says "you" to the user without a name and the user had not
  just spoken; `respond` could fire on remarks that need no reply. Measured offline only on the
  prompt; `copilot-detector-eval --script evals/bench/group_standup.toml --mode standup`
  (synthetic, no meeting text in the repository) measures it live. The button and the new type
  need desktop 0.1.5; the gateway sends older apps `other` instead.

### 2. Typed commands answered literally, and a stale cache hit

- **Evidence:** Q1, Q2, Q11; Q2 replayed a six-minute-old answer.
- **Root cause:** `_force` uses typed text as the question verbatim; the exact-repeat cache keys
  on the normalized question text alone, so a context-dependent text such as "answer" repeats.
- **Fix:** typed text that is a bare command to answer ("answer", "answer that", "answer every
  question", up to a few words with no question) is treated like an empty Enter: answer what was
  just heard. Forced questions never read or write the exact-repeat cache.
- **Expected gain:** the Ask box does what the user meant; no stale replies.
- **Risk:** low. A real one-word question would be answered from context instead; the heard text
  includes it anyway. Backend-only.

### 3. A work log stuck in "extracting" after a provider outage

- **Evidence:** 2026-10-21 log; `job_runs` failed ×5 with "no credits"; status unchanged for four
  days; that day's work is missing from every pack since.
- **Root cause:** only `ModelOutputError` marks a log `failed`; a job that exhausts its retries
  leaves the log as it was, and nothing re-enqueues it.
- **Fix:** mark the log `failed` when its extraction raises (any error, final attempt), and have
  the scheduler re-enqueue `failed` and stale `extracting` logs (older than an hour, at most once
  an hour each) so a provider outage heals itself. Then re-extract the 2026-10-21 log on Stage 0
  (one gpt-6.1-sol call, about $0.05; your call).
- **Expected gain:** the missing day's work back in memory; no silent gaps.
- **Risk:** low; retries are bounded, and extraction is idempotent by content hash. Backend-only.

### 4. Prepared standup points are unreadable live

- **Evidence:** Q9's points: four 150–300-character summaries, one narrating "the user" and
  "Claude"; 16 "yesterday" items in the pack.
- **Root cause:** `standup_pack` uses each node's full summary as a talking point and caps
  nothing; `worklog.extract` keeps the log's narration.
- **Fix:** prepared points from each node's title plus its state, at most about 12 words, the 4
  most significant (done PRs and tickets first), in the user's voice; the extraction prompt
  writes summaries as the user's own notes and never names an assistant or tool as the actor.
  The deep pass keeps the full pack.
- **Expected gain:** a glanceable update in the first 1.5 s.
- **Risk:** detail moves to the deep pass. Existing nodes keep their narration until their log is
  extracted again. Backend-only.

### 5. Post-call commitments have no due date and carry meta-text into the next pack

- **Evidence:** both commitments: empty `due_date`, one containing "the session date was not
  provided", one a misheard name.
- **Root cause:** no date in `postcall_request`; nothing tells the model to keep commitment text
  clean; no keyterms reach STT.
- **Fix:** add the session's local date and weekday to the post-call context; tell the model a
  commitment is the promise alone, in the user's words, with no remarks about the transcript;
  send the company's term, project and system names (at most 50) as Deepgram keyterms at session
  start.
- **Expected gain:** "today or tomorrow" becomes 2026-10-27; the next standup's commitments read
  cleanly; fewer misheard company terms.
- **Risk:** low. Keyterms cost $0.0013/min, already in the estimate. Backend-only.

### 6. "Yesterday" on a Monday after a missing Friday log

- **Evidence:** Q9 and Q10's deep answers said "Yesterday:" for Thursday's work.
- **Fix:** when the last log day is not the previous working day, the pack names it ("Last log:
  Thursday 2026-10-22; no log for Friday") and the standup rule says to say "on Thursday";
  week questions get the week's items.
- **Expected gain:** no misdated update. **Risk:** low. Backend-only.

### 7. Conversation summaries cut at the output cap

- **Evidence:** 5 of 16 summary calls returned exactly 500 output tokens.
- **Fix:** give the summary job reasoning headroom on OpenAI (as the deep pass has) and minimal
  effort; log the stop reason, and on `max_tokens` keep the previous summary plus the lines
  instead of a truncated one.
- **Expected gain:** later answers keep what was said earlier. **Risk:** low. Backend-only.

### 8. Detector and held-pass rates far above the cost estimate

- **Evidence:** about 930 detector calls and 400 held passes per hour against 250 and 20.
- **Fix:** update the estimate in ARCHITECTURE.md for talk-heavy meetings; before any Claude
  routing, gate held passes in standups and meetings on a question cue in the interim text.
- **Expected gain:** an honest estimate now; about $0.50/hour on Claude later. **Risk:** low.

### 9. Detector cold at session start

- **Evidence:** 3 first-token timeouts and a failed decision in the first two minutes.
- **Fix:** prewarm the detector route at session start alongside the fast pass.
- **Expected gain:** early questions not lost. **Risk:** one tiny call per session.

### Recommended order

1, 2 and 3 first (wrong answers, a broken input, lost data), then 5 and 4 (what the user reads
and what feeds the next standup), then 6, 7, 9; 8 is a document change. Only 1(a), the name at
work on the Work screen, needs a desktop release; the rest are backend-only.
